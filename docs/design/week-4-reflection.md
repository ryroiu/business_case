# Week 4 reflection — Signpost plan and tasks

**Draft for Ryan's review and personalization before submission.** This text describes the recorded AI-assisted planning process; it does not claim that human sign-off or user testing occurred.

## What went well?

The existing specification gave this assignment a clear starting point. Its requirement IDs made it possible to connect each planning decision and task to a specific purpose. Keeping Signpost focused on finding services, ownership, and supporting information also made technology choices straightforward: the existing Vue application, CSV data, and browser-based pins already fit the idea. AI assistance was most useful for organizing these decisions into the course templates and identifying where the task list needed more precise completion checks. Reviewing the current repository helped the documents reflect the prototype that actually exists.

## What did not go well?

The project had already moved into implementation during the earlier prototype assignment, so this was not a clean specification-to-plan-to-tasks sequence. A generic task list could easily have asked for features that were already built. The first drafts also needed refinement: review-date tests were too broad, participant scheduling came too late, and the release task needed an explicit dependency on any fixes discovered during evaluation. Another challenge was keeping evidence separate from assumptions. Earlier browser checks and fictional interview responses cannot establish that real users will find a runbook within 30 seconds. Human approval and real-user outcomes therefore remain pending instead of being presented as completed work.

## How might I approach this type of work in the future?

I would begin with a short inventory of existing features and open questions, then review the plan before developing the task list. I would give AI a specific brief containing the specification, current code, course guide, and boundaries of the assignment. I would ask for small tasks with clear dependencies and observable results, review each one, and make direct edits where the draft is vague or inconsistent. I would also arrange participants earlier and keep implementation checks separate from usability findings. Saving the prompts and initial drafts would make it easier to explain what changed and why. Before submission, I would personalize the reflection, record my own approval, and verify that the published document links open correctly.
